Hong Kong (long history, indices only)

Hong Kong publishes official price and rental indices of private flats from the same source since 1979, which makes it the longest consistent record among the cities studied here, with two large falls. There is no open register of sales, so the tests that need individual transactions are not run.

What moved the price-to-rent ratio

The user-cost formula takes today's interest rate as if it held for ever. This decomposition (Campbell and Shiller 1988; Campbell, Davis, Gallin and Martin 2009) lets rates and rents vary: the ratio can rise only if real interest rates are expected to be lower, if rents are expected to grow faster, or for another reason, which is whatever the first two leave unexplained. Sample: 1981 Q4 to 2025 Q1; interest rate: banks' best lending rate, minus inflation.

How much to trust it. The intervals are wide. A forecasting model estimated on a few decades understates how persistent rates and rents are, and what it then fails to explain lands in "the rest": on simulated samples of this length in which there is no premium at all, the method attributes 31% of the variations to it at the median, and up to 101% in one case out of twenty. The 104% found here is above that range. "The rest" is a residual: it also contains credit conditions, taxes and the model's errors.

Check with the interbank rate (added after the first results, because the best lending rate has barely moved since 2000 while borrowing costs have): the share of "the rest" is 93% on 1990–2025, and real rates again explain little.
