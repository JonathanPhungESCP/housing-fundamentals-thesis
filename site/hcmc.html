Ho Chi Minh City (exploratory)

Administrative land values, not market prices. These are the land prices set by the city for taxes, fees and compensation (Resolution 87/2025/NQ-HĐND of the HCMC People's Council, in force since 1 January 2026). They are neither transaction prices nor asking prices, they cover land only, and they have historically been well below market values. Vietnam publishes no transaction register and no rents, so the rent-capitalisation test run for Paris and Singapore cannot be run here; the two cross-checks further down use published city-wide averages instead.

Two cross-checks from published averages

The tests run on sales for Paris and Singapore need a register of transactions, which Vietnam does not publish. The two cross-checks can still be computed from city-wide averages read in brokers' and official publications. They use asking prices and averages, not medians of sales, and several assumptions; read them as orders of magnitude.

Income side: beyond capacity, whatever the case. A 60 m² flat costs 11 to 13 years of average household income, and 1.6 to 3.8 times what that household can borrow at mortgage rates of 10% to 14%. This result does not use the government yield.

Rent side: it depends on the discount rate. The check asks what else the buyer's money could earn. In Paris the government borrows at about the same rate as households, so the choice does not matter. In Vietnam the government borrows at 4.5% and households at 10% to 14%, and the answer changes with the rate. Incomes per person in the city grew by about 7.2% a year between 2010 and 2022; that is the ceiling for rents.

Not run for lack of data: quality-adjusted price index, explosiveness test, history of the ratios, sales volumes, cross-section of districts. Published averages differ widely with the definition (60 to 112 million VND/m² in 2026 depending on the source and on resale, new-build or newly launched projects), the city's boundary changed in July 2025, and the income survey may not capture all informal income. Household income is income per person times the national household size. No verdict is given for this city.

The average overstates the rise. Flats at or under 50 million VND/m² were close to half of sales in 2020 and 18% in 2024; flats above 4 billion VND went from 2% of sales in 2020 to 70% in the first nine months of 2024. Part of the increase in the average is a change in what is sold, which is what the quality-adjusted indices correct for in Paris and Singapore.

Of the markets studied, this is the only one where a stretched price-to-income ratio and falling sales are seen together today. In the four past cases found in Singapore and Paris, that combination was followed once by a fall of at least 10%, twice by smaller declines and once by nothing; it is a description of where the market stands, not a forecast.

60 m² resale flat at 60 million VND/m², 30% down, 35% of income over 25 years.

  • Credit was cheap when prices rose. At the introductory rates of 2025 a better-off household could finance the flat; at the rates applied once that period ends, it cannot. 84% of buyers of a home to live in say they need a loan.
  • Foreign buyers are few: 1,519 homes acquired nationwide since the 2023 law, in a city with close to 460,000 flats.
  • Money from abroad is large: 10.34 billion USD reached the city in 2025, of which about 22% goes to real estate. That would pay for about 16,000 such flats a year (computed here, at about 26,000 VND per dollar); not all of it buys flats.
  • About a third of buyers are investors: 67% / 30% / 4% (own home / to let / quick resale).

The buyers are therefore better-off households with savings, families pooling money including from abroad, investors, and households who borrowed at introductory rates. The share of each cannot be measured with these sources, and the gap between the richest fifth and the average is the national one, applied to the city.
